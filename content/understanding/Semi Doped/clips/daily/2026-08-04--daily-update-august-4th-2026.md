### SK Hynix and SanDisk Release First OCP High Bandwidth Flash Spec at FMS 2026

SK Hynix and SanDisk published the [first OCP technical specification for High Bandwidth Flash](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-and-sk-hynix-advance-global-standardization-high) on August 3, 2026, formally establishing HBF as a new memory-storage tier targeting AI inference workloads. The spec was developed through the Open Compute Project, with [Google and Tenstorrent backing the effort](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/04/sk-hynix-sandisk-debut-hbf-standard-to-challenge-ai-memory-bottlenecks-with-google-tenstorrent-support/), giving the standard immediate credibility across both hyperscaler and AI accelerator communities. FMS 2026 ran August 4-6 at the Santa Clara Convention Center, making it the public debut stage for a format the two companies have spent considerable engineering effort positioning between DRAM and conventional NAND.

The specification’s release through OCP provides the formal foundation that third-party silicon vendors need to build HBF-compatible controllers and interconnects. TrendForce reported the standard is aimed directly at AI memory bottlenecks that have grown acute as inference deployments scale. Kioxia used the same conference floor to press its own case in the tier, announcing the GP1 Series SSDs built on [XL-FLASH Gen 2 reaching 10 million random read IOPS](https://www.storagereview.com/news/kioxia-gp1-series-hits-10-million-random-read-iops-on-xl-flash-gen-2) and separately showing [PCIe 6.0 NVMe prototypes](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/112973/kioxia-showcases-next-gen-pcie-6-0-nvme-ssds-at-future-of-memory-and-storage/index.html). TrendForce noted Kioxia is also [eyeing PCIe 6.0 and UFS 5.0 mass production](https://www.trendforce.com/news/2026/08/04/news-kioxia-steps-up-ai-nand-competition-with-south-korean-rivals-eyes-pcie-6-0-and-ufs-5-0-mass-production/) as it works to close ground on its South Korean rivals.

On an adjacent front:

  * Marvell announced it would [showcase AI memory and storage infrastructure for agentic inference](https://investor.marvell.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1028/marvell-to-showcase-advanced-ai-memory-and-storage-infrastructure-portfolio-for-agentic-ai-inference-at-fms-2026) at FMS 2026, adding its silicon portfolio to the week’s converging narrative.

  * Credo Technology exhibited [interconnect products for AI memory and storage applications](https://investors.credosemi.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Credo-to-Showcase-AI-Memory-and-Storage-Connectivity-Solutions-at-FMS-2026/default.aspx) at the show, targeting the connectivity layer that HBF and high-IOPS SSDs both require.

  * Silicon Motion presented [next-generation NAND flash controllers for agentic AI and physical AI applications](https://siliconmotiontechnologycorporation.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/silicon-motion-showcases-next-generation-storage-solutions), spanning workloads from AI factories down to edge deployments.

### Tower Lifts Its 2028 Model by $800 Million as onsemi Guides AI Data Center Revenue to Double

Tower Semiconductor reported [record second quarter revenue of $460 million](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/04/3338165/0/en/Tower-Semiconductor-Announces-Record-Results-for-Revenue-and-Profitability-for-the-Second-Quarter-2026-with-Third-Quarter-2026-Record-Revenue-Guidance.html), up 24% year over year, and guided third quarter revenue to $520 million, a 31% year-over-year increase that would set another company record. Silicon photonics carried the quarter: SiPho revenue reached a $680 million annual run rate, up from $180 million a year earlier, with Tower expecting to cross $1 billion in run rate during the fourth quarter. Net profit excluding non-recurring items was $91 million, a 20% net margin and 95% above the $47 million posted a year ago.

The more consequential number is the long-range model. In May, Tower [told investors it was targeting](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/928876/000117891326002607/exhibit_99-1.htm) $2.8 billion of revenue and $750 million of net profit in 2028. Three months later CEO Russell Ellwanger raised that to “$3.6 billion of revenue with $1.2 billion net profit, being fully spoken for by our customers.” That is an $800 million revenue raise and a 60% increase in the profit target inside a single quarter, on capacity the company says is already committed.

**onsemi** described the same demand from the power side a day earlier. Revenue of [$1,604 million](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/03/3337876/0/en/onsemi-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results.html) grew 9% year over year and 6% sequentially, non-GAAP gross margin reached 39.3%, and free cash flow of $425.4 million quadrupled from a year ago. CEO Hassane El-Khoury said “AI data center remains our fastest-growing business, and we now expect revenue to more than double in 2026.” Third quarter revenue is guided to $1.65 billion to $1.75 billion, and the company pointed to an expanded role in the Nvidia MGX ecosystem, EliteSiC and MOSFET wins with China cloud power supplier Great Wall, and the launch of GaNEXUS, its 40V to 650V gallium nitride portfolio.
