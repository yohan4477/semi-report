### AMD to Buy Taalas

AMD has agreed to acquire Taalas, a Toronto-based AI inference chip startup, in a move that extends the chipmaker’s push into purpose-built silicon for running AI models. Taalas was co-founded by Ljubisa Bajic, a former AMD executive who also served as CEO of Tenstorrent, and the Taalas team will join AMD under Vamsi Boppana’s AI group. The company’s focus is chips designed to accelerate individual AI models rather than serve as general-purpose accelerators, a distinction AMD is now actively building toward, according to [ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/amd-to-acquire-taalas-for-model-specific-ai-inference-chips/).

The acquisition comes weeks after AMD’s Cerebras-adjacent moves and lands shortly after a strong financial quarter. Bajic’s background straddles both AMD’s own history and the broader AI chip startup world, which makes the hire as notable as the deal itself. [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/advanced-micro-devices-to-buy-startup-taalas-for-new-ai-chips) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/amd-deepens-ai-inference-bet-with-taalas-deal-chip-race-heats-up-2026-08-06/) both reported the acquisition; financial terms were not disclosed.

> _**Vik** : There is an emerging trend of hardcoding AI models into silicon, which is the approach that Taalas pitches. Interestingly, Google is also developing an experimental custom chip called [Frozen V2](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/20/alphabet-googl-stock-ai-chip-report.html) that is designed to embed parts of Gemini directly into silicon. It is aimed for a 2028 rollout with 6 to 10x better power efficiency than TPUs._

### SK Hynix Commits 54 Trillion Won Across Two Fabs for AI Memory

SK Hynix announced it will invest [54 trillion won across two South Korean fabrication facilities](https://news.skhynix.com/en/fab-facility-investment-2026/), splitting the commitment between 35.2 trillion won for the Yongin “Y2” fab and 19.1 trillion won for the Cheongju “M17” facility. The company framed the decision as a move to “seize opportunities in line with the market’s growth speed” and to support stability in the global AI semiconductor supply chain. M17’s cleanroom is scheduled to [open in December 2028](https://news.skhynix.com/en/fab-facility-investment-2026/), with Y2 following in [June 2029](https://news.skhynix.com/en/fab-facility-investment-2026/), meaning the capacity additions are timed well into the next decade. Both sites will expand production of DRAM and NAND, with output calibrated to customer demand rather than speculative builds. 

The dual-fab commitment stands as the largest single memory CapEx announcement of the current cycle.

> _**Vik** : SK hynix’s fab expansions are a high-stakes bet on sustained AI memory growth. For this kind of capacity expansion to make sense, sustained demand for memory needs to continue through to 2028 or 2029, which is an eternity if you consider the current pace of AI. There is a nonzero possibility that some new memory technique or tier emerges between now and then, rendering this fab capacity or less in-demand than it is today_

### SpaceX, Tesla and Nvidia Back Captive Chip and Data Center Buildouts

SpaceX and Tesla plan to [spend $16.8 billion initially](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacex-says-terafab-be-built-texas-with-initial-investment-168-billion-2026-08-06/) on the Terafab chip plant in Texas, a facility where SpaceX will also [build natural gas power plants](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/spacex-to-build-natural-gas-power-plants-for-texas-chip-factory) to supply the site’s energy needs directly. The vertical integration is thorough by design: the same companies consuming the chips are paying to manufacture them, collapsing the distance between silicon and deployment. That logic is finding company in Australia, where data center firm Firmus Technologies has closed a [$2 billion fundraising round](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-07/ai-data-center-group-firmus-draws-2-billion-from-coatue-nvidia) backed by Nvidia and Coatue, among others.

Firmus nearly doubled its valuation to [over $10.5 billion](https://www.reuters.com/technology/firmus-nearly-doubles-valuation-over-105-billion-4-months-with-nvidia-backed-2026-08-07/) in the Nvidia-backed raise, with Jane Street also joining the investor group. The round gives Nvidia a direct stake in the compute infrastructure running its own chips, a structural position rather than a customer relationship. Firmus is building AI data center capacity in Australia, a market investors are treating as distinct from the crowded North American pipeline. The two deals, separated by an ocean, are drawing from the same logic: own the stack.

Also moving:

  * Nscale, a European GPU cloud provider, is [targeting a September US IPO](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-06/nscale-touts-51-billion-in-contracts-targets-september-us-ipo) and claims $51 billion in contracts, showing independent data center operators are still racing to list before the window narrows.




### Optical Component Earnings and Fiber Capacity Bets Show AI Supply Chain Monetizing

Applied Optoelectronics and MACOM both reported [second-quarter and fiscal-third-quarter results](https://investors.ao-inc.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-optoelectronics-reports-second-quarter-2026-results) on August 6, 2026, adding to a growing pile of evidence that AI infrastructure spending has reached the optical supply chain in earnest. MACOM, reporting for the quarter ended July 3, joins AOI in what has become a crowded earnings calendar for photonics-adjacent names. 

GlobalFoundries posted [revenue of $1.786 billion for its second quarter](https://www.eetimes.com/globalfoundries-growth-makes-the-case-for-a-u-s-photonics-buildout/) and used the results to strengthen the commercial rationale for its U.S. government-backed silicon photonics investment.

Taken together, the earnings prints and capital programs trace a supply chain where optical components are now clearly moving from commodity inputs to priced scarcity.

> _**Vik** : Investors are rotating out of memory and into optical names. The recent drama with proposed chinese optical transceiver bans has sparked fear into investors as they understood how important optics is for the AI buildout. Expect this growing interest to continue._
