### Marvell Earnings Beat Fails to Calm Google Revenue Timing Fears

Marvell Technology posted a strong fiscal second quarter and [raised its annual revenue forecast](https://www.reuters.com/technology/marvell-technology-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-above-estimates-2026-08-27/), yet shares fell as investors fixated on a single uncomfortable question: when does the reported Google ASIC commitment actually show up in reported revenue. The anxiety isn’t idle. The Next Platform reports the [widely cited $120 billion figure](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/08/27/peeling-apart-that-supposed-120-billion-chip-deal-google-inked-with-marvell/5292984) reflects a multi-year cumulative estimate, not a near-term booking, which leaves a sizable gap between the headline number and anything Marvell can recognize soon. Q2 revenue came in at $1.87 billion, beating consensus, with data center sales carrying the bulk of the upside. CEO Matt Murphy pointed to accelerating custom silicon demand as the engine behind raised guidance, but analyst questions on the earnings call kept returning to Google deal pacing. The beat was real. The timeline remains genuinely murky.

### Anthropic Commits $45B to Nscale Compute, Drops MatX Buyout

Anthropic has [signed a six-year, $45 billion compute agreement](https://constructionreviewonline.com/anthropic-nscale-seal-a-45-billion-six-year-ai-deal-at-the-west-virginias-monarch-compute-campus/) with British infrastructure firm Nscale, anchored at the Monarch Compute Campus in Mason County, West Virginia, securing an reported 460MW of Nvidia Vera Rubin capacity. The deal gives Anthropic guaranteed GPU access without owning the silicon. That calculus apparently shaped its separate decision on MatX: talks toward a [$7 billion outright acquisition](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/08/28/exclusive-anthropic-planned-then-abandoned-7-billion-purchase-of-matx-sources-say) of the chip startup collapsed, and Anthropic is now pursuing a partnership instead, according to sources cited by Reuters. The company had weighed buying MatX partly to reduce Nvidia dependence, but backed away. Anthropic is expected to publicly file its IPO prospectus after Labor Day.

### SK Hynix Breaks Ground on $4 Billion Indiana HBM Fab, Production Set for 2029

SK Hynix held a groundbreaking ceremony on August 27 at Purdue University’s Holloway Gymnasium in West Lafayette, Indiana, marking the start of construction on its [first U.S. AI memory production hub](https://news.skhynix.com/en/indiana-groundbreaking-ceremony-sketch/), a facility spanning roughly 133 acres that will house an HBM production line alongside advanced packaging R&D. CEO Kwak Noh-Jung told Reuters the Indiana fab won’t begin AI chip output until 2029, and that a memory shortage is expected to persist through 2030. The project carries a $4 billion price tag, according to Bloomberg. Kwak also said the company is exploring deeper cooperation with Japanese customers and suppliers, including potential closer ties with Kioxia in NAND, extending SK Hynix’s strategic reach across both major memory segments.

### Nvidia Pauses Cloud Revenue Deals as Memory Costs Squeeze Margins

Nvidia has paused revenue-sharing arrangements with AI cloud partners, according to a Wall Street Journal report cited by Reuters, a move that reshapes the financial terms underpinning its hyperscaler relationships. The timing is awkward: the company [lowered its gross-margin outlook](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=13401) in its fiscal 2027 second-quarter earnings, citing surging memory-chip prices that are pushing up costs. Nvidia also warned that revenue growth will trail demand growth, blaming supply-chain bottlenecks spanning foundry capacity, data-center power, and available sites. Both pressures arrive together, and together they put the cost structure of Nvidia’s cloud business under a degree of scrutiny it hasn’t faced in its recent run of outsized quarters.

### OpenAI Jalapeño Benchmarks Land as Georgia Power Deal Clears Review

First independent benchmarks for OpenAI’s Jalapeño ASIC show 1.5 to 1.9 times better efficiency per watt than Nvidia’s current inference hardware, according to EE Times. Sam Altman’s summary was characteristically spare: “We made a chip and it is fast.” The chip is a clean-sheet general-purpose AI accelerator, not a derivative design, which makes the efficiency figures meaningful rather than the usual apples-to-oranges marketing claim. On the infrastructure side, the Georgia Public Service Commission approved Georgia Power’s power contract for [OpenAI’s $20 billion data center](https://www.ajc.com/business/2026/08/georgia-power-deal-to-power-openai-data-center-near-savannah-is-approved/), with commissioners adding consumer-protection provisions requiring OpenAI to absorb cost overruns rather than passing them to residential ratepayers. Site plan review is the next procedural step before construction can advance.

### TSMC Widens Foundry Lead as AI Chip Demand Lifts Market 29%

The global foundry market expanded 29% in Q2 on surging demand for AI silicon, lifting total revenue well above year-earlier levels and handing TSMC a share lead it continues to stretch. Samsung, by contrast, sits at roughly [7% market share](https://biz.chosun.com/en/en-it/2026/08/28/LMDHJ4PEKZAPDGBSK5YA3KOIDI/), a gap wide enough to raise real questions about its advanced-node roadmap. TSMC’s dominance isn’t an accident of timing: years of disciplined capacity investment in leading-edge nodes and CoWoS advanced packaging have made it the default supplier for every major AI chip designer. That position gives TSMC considerable leverage heading into 2027 capacity allocation talks, where demand for CoWoS slots already outpaces available supply.
