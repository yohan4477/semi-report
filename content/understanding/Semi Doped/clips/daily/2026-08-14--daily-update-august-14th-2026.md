### Applied Materials Posts Record Quarter as Equipment Cycle Broadens

Applied Materials reported [record revenue of $9.12 billion](https://ir.appliedmaterials.com/news-releases/news-release-details/applied-materials-announces-third-quarter-2026-results) for its third quarter of 2026, up 25 percent year over year, with non-GAAP EPS climbing 41 percent to $3.50. The company’s EPIC Center R&D partnerships expanded to 11 engagements, even as shares slipped after the guidance cleared a bar that the market had already priced in. Reuters noted that the [forecast came in above analyst estimates](https://www.reuters.com/business/applied-materials-forecasts-quarterly-revenue-above-estimates-2026-08-13/) but investors sold the news, a familiar dynamic when expectations run ahead of results.

Lam Research added ballast to the cycle by committing to [invest more than $3 billion to expand its global lab network](https://investor.lamresearch.com/2026-08-13-Lam-Research-Announces-Plans-to-Invest-More-than-3B-to-Expand-Global-Lab-Network,-Increase-Innovation-Velocity-in-the-AI-Era), framing the move explicitly around AI-era innovation velocity. Korean supplier ISTE provided a ground-level read on the same trend: the company posted [record quarterly revenue of approximately 16.85 billion won](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=13050), a 401 percent jump from a year earlier, with operating profit turning positive at 2.07 billion won.

> _**Vik:** WFE equipment vendors have visibility into 8-quarter rolling window as customers providers provide roadmaps out to 2030. This is unusual for this industry, which usually looks ahead only 2-3 quarters. The demand for semiconductors remain strong._
> 
> _A note on ISTE and what they do: they provide cleaning materials for front opening unified pods (FOUPs), specifically for HBM. These FOUPs are extremely clean “boxes” that transport wafers between machines in a fab on ceiling mounted rails. And they do need internal cleaning. I find it interesting how far down the AI supply chain goes._
> 
> _**Austin:** The 8-quarter window exists so everyone downstream has time to add capacity. Applied’s CFO Brice Hill said the company is “systematically translating our 8-quarter customer demand forecast into a consolidated signal to our suppliers,” and that Applied has on the order of 2,000 direct suppliers!!! No wonder they need two years._
> 
> _Asked whether the visibility is shifting order patterns, prepayments or pricing, Hill said deposits exist but “not across the board”, pricing runs as 2 to 3 year project contracts that move “relatively slowly”, and there’s “no change in the overall model.”_
> 
>  _This is in contrast to substrates. Ibiden’s ¥500 billion expansion is customer-funded, its stated policy being to “receive an amount equivalent to the investment as an advance payment”._
> 
> _So when Intel says it’s “actively securing supply of substrates and memory”, securing can mean funding the plant, not just placing an order. Not the same when Intel says it’s “aggressively locking in tool purchase orders”. It might seem like substrate makers have more power than tool vendors if they can ask customers to prepay, but I think it’s the opposite; putting more financial risk on the customers like this isn’t great and comes at a cost, I’d rather be AMAT and not need to do this to my customers._

### SanDisk Investor Day Bets on NAND Boom Through Decade’s End

At its 2026 Investor Day, SanDisk laid out a financial model projecting [mid-to-high-teens revenue growth](https://www.reuters.com/business/sandisk-forecasts-mid-to-high-teens-revenue-growth-through-2030-2026-08-13/) from FY2028 through FY2030, anchored by what the company described as a leading NAND technology roadmap and durable customer partnerships. The plan goes further than growth targets alone: SanDisk committed to [returning 100 percent of excess cash](https://investor.sandisk.com/news-releases/news-release-details/sandisk-details-growth-strategy-and-long-term-financial-model) to shareholders after capital investment, a pledge that landed well with investors already watching memory prices climb. Asian memory chipmakers rose on the back of the SanDisk presentation, with Bloomberg reporting the outlook [lifted broader confidence](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-14/asian-memory-chipmakers-rise-as-sandisk-outlook-lifts-confidence) across the sector.

See the full investor day 98-slide presentation [here](https://investor.sandisk.com/static-files/c050c0a0-91fe-4186-bb34-3b331378060f).

### Chinese Foundries and CXMT Post Records as Domestic Chip Stack Takes Shape

SMIC, China’s largest contract chipmaker, posted its [first-ever quarterly revenue above $3 billion](https://www.businesstoday.com.my/2026/08/14/smic-posts-record-revenue-beyond-us3-billion-on-ai-boom/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=smic-posts-record-revenue-beyond-us3-billion-on-ai-boom) in Q2 2026, with net profit [jumping 261.7 percent year on year to $479.2 million](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3363929/ai-demand-drives-triple-digit-quarterly-profit-growth-chinese-foundries-smic-hua-hong?utm_source=rss_feed) on surging orders for mature-node chips used alongside AI processors. Demand ran so far ahead of forecasts that management is now [actively evaluating additional equipment installations](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3363996/smic-weighs-more-capacity-ai-related-chip-demand-exceeds-forecasts?utm_source=rss_feed) across its fabs, and the company has begun raising wafer prices as capacity tightens. Smaller rival Hua Hong Grace saw first-half net profit [surge 404 percent to $59.5 million](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/finance/article/339862/Hua-Hong-Grace-net-profit-jumps-404pc-to-US595-mn-in-first-half-of-2026); Digitimes attributed the swing largely to pricing power, noting its fabs were already running full before rates moved.

CXMT, the domestic DRAM maker, overtook Tencent to become China’s most valuable listed company [just 17 days after its IPO, reaching a market cap of $524 billion](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cxmt-overtakes-tencent-to-become-chinas-most-valuable-company-17-days-after-its-ipo). Korean media reported that CXMT’s latest DRAM yield rate trails Samsung’s [by only two percentage points](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026081409064935177), a claim that, if accurate, narrows the technology gap considerably.

Behind the demand:

  * China’s AI chip market is [approaching 90 percent domestic share in 2026](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260812VL213/market-2026-ai-chip-nvidia-huawei.html), with Huawei on course to displace Nvidia as the leading supplier, according to Digitimes.

  * CXMT’s market capitalization now [rivals Intel’s](https://www.businessinsider.com/cxmt-stock-price-market-cap-intel-tencent-ai-chips-2026-8), according to Business Insider.

### Anthropic Pursues Decart Acquisition

Anthropic is in talks to [acquire Nvidia-backed Decart AI for $6 billion](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-08-13/anthropic-said-in-talks-to-buy-ai-startup-decart-for-6-billion), according to Bloomberg, a deal that would add inference-focused model capability to the company’s portfolio as it prepares for a public listing. Decart, whose backers include Nvidia, has drawn Anthropic’s interest partly because of its work on fast, efficient inference infrastructure. The acquisition talks arrive as Anthropic separately targets [77% gross margins before its IPO](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324234/20260813/anthropic-talks-acquire-decart-6b-targeting-77-gross-margins-before-ipo.htm), a threshold that would make the financial story considerably cleaner for prospective public investors. The company has set its sights on what would be a [$2 trillion IPO valuation](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/324347/20260813/anthropic-targets-2-trillion-ipo-record-export-control-that-froze-june-revenue-remains-active.htm), which would set a record if achieved.
