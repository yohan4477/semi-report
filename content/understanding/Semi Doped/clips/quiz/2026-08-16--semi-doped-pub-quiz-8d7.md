In 1991, company X bought a small email program written at the [University of Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_Urbana-Champaign) by a staffer named [Steve Dorner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Dorner). He had called it [Eudora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eudora_\(email_client\)), after the writer [Eudora Welty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eudora_Welty), because her story "[Why I Live at the P.O.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_I_Live_at_the_P.O.)" is about a woman who moves into a post office, and what is email but a mailbox that follows you home? Welty, told that her name now rode on a piece of software, reportedly found the whole thing mysterious but was flattered anyway. X kept Eudora until 2018, when it handed the code to the Computer History Museum. 

**Name X.**
