This process takes a material that barely conducts electricity and turns it into something useful, by adding foreign atoms at concentrations as low as one for every hundred million. In a chip fab, that means injecting boron or phosphorus atoms into pure silicon. The word for this is the exact same word officials use when an Olympic skater fails a drug test, and the exact same word an engineer uses when building the transistor in your phone. In electronics, a material that's had this treatment is no longer called "pure." It gets a new label: extrinsic. The heavier the dose, the more the material starts acting like a plain conductor instead of a semiconductor. 

**Name the process.**

## **Answer:[Doping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doping)**

Etymology: The word "dope" comes from the Dutch word _**doop**_ , which means "sauce" or "dipping liquid". Over time, Dutch settlers in early America used _doop_ to mean thick sauces, gravies, or sweet syrups, which eventually evolved into the modern English word "dope" and its various historical and slang meanings.
