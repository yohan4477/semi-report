In the spring of 1918, a [U.S. Army Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Signal_Corps) captain stationed in Paris was given a wartime problem: find a way to detect [German shortwave transmissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutscher_Kurzwellensender_Atlantik) that existing receivers couldn't tune into reliably. He later said the solution came to him while walking home from watching an air raid, mixing an incoming signal with a second frequency to produce a lower, easier-to-amplify difference frequency. 

He filed the patent from Paris in 1919. That wartime circuit, built for military eavesdropping rather than entertainment, is now the basic architecture inside nearly every AM/FM radio, TV tuner, and the RF front end of a smartphone. 

**Who was this officer?**
