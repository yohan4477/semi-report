### TSMC Locks 2027 Price Hikes as EDA Vendors Certify A14 Flows

TSMC has confirmed [3-6% wafer price increases](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260924PD214/tsmc-wafer-price-2027-demand.html) taking effect in 2027, squeezing chip designers even as the foundry accelerates its A14 node toward a 2028 production target. Cadence and Synopsys have both certified A14 design flows, with [Synopsys expanding its TSMC partnership](https://news.synopsys.com/2026-09-23-Synopsys-and-TSMC-Partner-to-Accelerate-AI-Systems-Innovation-with-Agentic-AI-and-Advanced-Design) to cover A14 IP support and co-packaged optics alongside agentic AI design tools. Siemens completed its own A14 flow certification through an expanded TSMC collaboration announced via the Siemens Newsroom. IC-Link by imec joined TSMC’s Open Innovation Platform Cloud Alliance as the first managed-cloud service supporting mixed-partner EDA flows, giving European fabless companies and research groups cloud-based access to advanced-node design infrastructure across all three certified toolchains.

> **Vik:** _3-6% and nobody’s pushing back. Order visibility runs to 2030 now, so designers have no room to argue - where else are they going to go? TSMC raises because it can. This means that a whole lot of other fabs like Samsung, UMC, and VIS can raise prices._

### Qualcomm Locks In Apple License Renewal, Splits Snapdragon 8 Flagship Into Two

Qualcomm has [renewed its global patent licensing agreement with Apple, effective April 2027](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-24/qualcomm-renews-global-licensing-pact-with-apple-starting-2027), securing a continued revenue floor from the iPhone maker just as its modem business faces longer-term uncertainty. The chip company also used Snapdragon Summit 2026 in Maui on September 22 to unveil a two-track flagship lineup: the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 and a standard Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6, designed around physically distinct dies to serve different smartphone tiers. Both chips are manufactured exclusively at TSMC, according to [The Elec](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14172). CEO Cristiano Amon introduced High Bandwidth Compute technology, borrowed from data-center architecture, as a feature of the new mobile platform, alongside the Adreno Neural Fusion graphics engine targeting console-level gaming performance.

> **Vik:** _HBC in a phone is the part I’d watch because the main benefit comes from the extremely low pJ/bit it takes to move data between memory and logic. HBC stacks DRAM on the logic die to get bandwidth and skip CoWoS, but the packaging pain doesn’t vanish - it just moves to 3D die stacking, the harder version.[I wrote about that shift here.](https://www.viksnewsletter.com/p/qualcomms-high-bandwidth-compute)_

### DeepSeek Turns to Huawei Ascend for Frontier-Scale Model Training

DeepSeek is planning to train an [8-trillion-parameter model on Huawei chips](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/107303/deepseek-bets-on-training-ai-models-with-huawei-chips-plans-8-trillion-parameter-model), according to Seoul Economic Daily sourcing cited by The Elec, marking a concrete commitment to domestic silicon at a scale no Chinese lab has publicly attempted. The hardware of choice is Huawei’s Atlas 960E SuperPoD, a cluster packing [4,096 NPUs and 8 EFLOPS of FP8 compute](https://pandaily.com/huawei-atlas-960e-superpod-npo-hi-one-4096-npus-8-eflops) into a single NPO/Hi-ONE optical-fabric node. That spec puts it in direct competition with Nvidia’s H100-class systems, which U.S. export controls have effectively locked out of China’s frontier AI market. Huawei built the 960E without EUV lithography, a constraint that once looked crippling and now looks like a solved engineering problem.

> **Vik:** _DeepSeek’s training right now is 2 trillion params, so this is a big jump on silicon they’ve never trained a frontier model on. Huge bet, reportedly._

### Microsoft and Meta Push Hyperscale Spending Into New Geographic and Financial Territory

[Microsoft plans $10 billion-plus in Gulf investment](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/microsoft-plans-10-billion-plus-gulf-investment-with-focus-resilience-2026-09-23/) across four Persian Gulf countries through 2030, covering infrastructure buildout and cybersecurity, with President Brad Smith announcing an additional $2 billion tranche as the first significant regional update since the U.S.-Iran conflict. The commitment leans explicitly on resilience as a design principle, not just capacity. Meta, for its part, has set a [2026 capital expenditure target of $145 billion](https://fortune.com/2026/09/24/meta-145-billion-capex-military-budgets/), a figure that exceeds every national military budget on earth except those of the U.S., China, and Russia. That number spans a 1 GW Ohio data center already operating, a 5 GW Louisiana facility in planning, and a proposed transoceanic subsea cable at petabit capacity. BlackRock and IFM are separately closing in on a $25 billion Asia data center deal.
