### Musk Confirms Intel Stays in Terafab as TSMC Operating Role Ends

Elon Musk has ruled out TSMC taking any operational role at Terafab, the Texas AI chip complex he is building, saying [his own companies will construct and run the facility](https://www.aninews.in/news/business/elon-musk-rules-out-wider-tsmc-role-in-texas-terafab-says-his-companies-will-build-and-run-facility20261007151640) while TSMC contributes process technology under license. The clarification came after Intel shares initially slid on fears Musk might hand TSMC a broader position, then rebounded once the structure became clearer. Bloomberg confirmed that Intel will keep working on the Terafab venture, preserving its place in a project that had briefly looked like it might sideline the company entirely. Terafab thus takes shape as a Musk-owned fab running TSMC process nodes with Intel in a [supporting capacity, according to Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-07/intel-to-keep-working-on-elon-musk-s-terafab-chipmaking-venture).

### SpaceX Pursues $40B Apollo Financing to Stockpile Nvidia Chips

SpaceX is in talks to [borrow $40 billion, led by Apollo](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/spacex-seeks-40-billion-buy-nvidia-chips-ft-reports-2026-10-06/), specifically to purchase Nvidia chips for its AI buildout. The scale is hard to ignore: forty billion dollars in debt, raised not for rockets or satellites but for GPUs. Doosan Enerbility has separately completed production of a [380-megawatt gas turbine](https://www.thelec.net/news/articleView.html?idxno=14429) destined directly for SpaceXAI, holding a shipment ceremony at its Changwon headquarters with roughly 70 attendees. SpaceX is sourcing chips through private credit and commissioning bespoke power infrastructure without routing either through a hyperscaler. The financing round, if closed, would rank among the largest single AI hardware purchases on record.

> **Vik:** _They’re building the whole stack themselves. Chips through private credit, and a 380MW Doosan turbine shipping straight to SpaceXAI, no hyperscaler in between. Compute’s still short, so owning your own supply makes sense right now._
> 
> **Austin:** _At Jensen’s $40B per GW for Vera Rubin, this loan buys roughly 1 GW of compute. One 380MW turbine is a start, but they’ll need a lot more power than that._

### Penguin Solutions lifts fiscal 2027 revenue target to $2.43 billion

Penguin Solutions raised its fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to [$2.43 billion](https://mugglehead.com/penguin-solutions-fiscal-2027-sales-outlook-q4-results/), up from a prior target of $2.2 billion, after reporting a Q4 earnings and revenue beat driven by demand for AI systems integration and memory subsystems. The company now guides for fiscal Q1 2026 revenue between $480 million and $520 million, with full-year adjusted operating margins expected to expand alongside the revenue ramp. The stock climbed on the news. Analyst reactions split: Stifel raised its price target on the strength of the revised guidance, while Barclays [cut its rating](https://www.barrons.com/articles/penguin-solutions-earnings-stock-price-f3e3c467), citing valuation concerns as the revenue trajectory steepens. The divergence puts investors in the familiar spot of deciding whether the growth is worth the multiple.

> **Vik:** _Memory is doing the heavy lifting here. Integrated Memory was over nine-tenths of the year over year gain, so this is a memory subsystem story dressed up as AI systems._

### Samsung Electro-Mechanics Cements AI Server MLCC Lead With Fresh $216M Order

Samsung Electro-Mechanics has [landed a $216 million MLCC contract](https://mkbn.mk.co.kr/news/english/12170014) for AI server supply, adding to a position that already covers more than 40% of the AI server MLCC market, according to Seoul Economic Daily. The company now holds over 40% share in AI server MLCCs, a component that has become a chokepoint in next-generation server builds as GPU-dense configurations demand far higher capacitor counts per board than conventional rack servers. Digitimes reports that the new deal locks in [2027 delivery commitments](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20261006VL216/ai-server-semco-mlcc-2027-demand.html) as supply pressure in the segment continues to build, with lead times tightening across the MLCC tier. The $216 million figure represents a discrete order on top of existing volume, not a cumulative tally, reinforcing how concentrated procurement has become around a single supplier.

> **Vik:** _600K MLCCs in a single rack. That’s where the demand is coming from._

### Applied Materials and Intel formalize next-generation transistor and packaging R&D pact

Applied Materials and Intel have [announced a formal R&D collaboration](https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/applied-materials-intel-deepen-rd-collaboration-on-transistor-scaling-and-advanced-packaging/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=applied-materials-intel-deepen-rd-collaboration-on-transistor-scaling-and-advanced-packaging) targeting next-generation transistors, interconnects, and advanced packaging technologies, drawing on Applied’s EPIC Center in Silicon Valley and Intel’s R&D campus in Hillsboro, Oregon. The goal is to shorten time-to-market for semiconductor processes central to AI computing. The two companies will combine capabilities across both facilities to accelerate chipmaking innovations that span transistor scaling and advanced packaging in a single coordinated program. Applied and Intel describe the relationship as built on decades of prior work together, now structured to move faster at the leading edge. Besi and Applied have separately [deepened their own packaging collaboration](https://bits-chips.com/article/besi-and-applied-deepen-packaging-collaboration/) around the same period, extending Applied’s co-development reach across the packaging supply chain.

> **Vik:** _The Applied-Intel headline is Foveros and transistors, but the Besi extension is the part I’d watch - die-on-panel and photonics-enabled interconnect._
> 
> **Austin:** _Intel joins Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix, TSMC, and Kioxia at EPIC. All three leading-edge foundries doing R &D at Applied’s site now. Good spot for Applied to be in._

### AI-Powered Hack Hits Seven South Korean Banks, Exposes 68,000 Records

Hackers used a Chinese AI cybersecurity tool called Artex AI to breach at least seven South Korean financial firms and steal personal information belonging to 68,000 people, South Korean officials said. The attacks were initially detected last week, and the National Police Agency’s cyber terror unit opened a formal probe on Tuesday. Investigators said the intrusion is one of the first known AI-agent-assisted attacks on the global financial system.
