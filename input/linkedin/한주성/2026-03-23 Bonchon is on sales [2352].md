<Bonchon is on sales>

When I worked at KPMG New York, 32nd Street in Koreatown was a weekly ritual. Fridays, we would head there to decompress over Korean fried chicken. Sometimes I'd ride the subway home with a takeout bag. That was Bonchon.

It's now on the market for sales.
Asking price: ~$220M.

🍗 From a 10-table shop in Busan to 486 locations across 10 countries

Founder Seo Jin-deok started in 2002 in a tiny storefront. After an earlier F&B failure, he pivoted to fried chicken — spending $100K on sauce development alone. In 2006, with $1,000 and almost no English, he flew to the US. He'd failed before. There was no going back.
He drove 14–15 hours across the country to manage franchisees personally. That persistence built word-of-mouth. Mark Zuckerberg became a Sunnyvale regular — and Bonchon became a brand.

Korean PE firm VIG Partners acquired control in 2018: relocated HQ from NYC to Dallas, rebuilt IT, expanded delivery. Revenue grew from $9M to $32M in six years. 486 stores across 10 countries.

One detail worth noting: there is not a single Bonchon store in Korea. Seo closed the last Korean location in 2016 — he read the market limits early and changed the stage entirely. A brand Korea doesn't know became Zuckerberg's go-to in California.

📊 Why is a Korean F&B company getting a 20x EBITDA multiple?

Korean F&B M&A typically trades at 4–6x EV/EBITDA. US franchise M&A runs 15–20x. Three reasons Bonchon gets the American multiple:
The battlefield is America. 60%+ of revenue is US-based. William Blair was selected as banker specifically to frame this as an American business to American buyers.

The white space is enormous. Buyers aren't paying for today's $10M EBITDA — they're paying for hundreds of untapped cities across Europe, Asia-Pacific, and beyond.

The buyer pool is global. 100 IOIs reportedly received. Global revenue unlocks global competition for the deal.

If Seo had stayed in Korea, Bonchon would be worth $30M, not $220M.

Where you choose to compete determines your enterprise value.

💡 A signal Korean F&B can't ignore
Korea has 600+ chicken brands. Population is shrinking. Trends cycle every six months. In that environment, your multiple is capped at 5x — permanently. Brands that don't go global will increasingly struggle to find exit buyers.

And to be honest: is Bonchon's chicken exceptional? No. Korean fried chicken — what you actually eat in Korea — is in a completely different league.
And yet Bonchon wins globally.

Every foreign visitor to Korea now puts Korean fried chicken on their itinerary. Imagine what happens when a truly great Korean chicken brand takes that product seriously to global markets.

Bonchon is worth $220M not because of the recipe. It's worth $220M because it chose the bigger stage - something that Korean F&B should note.
