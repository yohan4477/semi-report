<6 Years as a Franchisee. Then They Bought the Brand>

Korean fried-chicken brand Bonchon has been sold to Thailand’s Minor International, marking VIG Partners’ exit roughly eight years after its investment.

But what makes this deal interesting isn’t the exit. It’s who the buyer is.

In 2019, Minor acquired Bonchon’s operator in Thailand. In 2020, it secured the master franchise rights. After operating the business for years, it has now acquired the global brand itself.

Franchisee → Master Franchisee → Global Brand Owner.

Minor is not simply a local franchise operator. It is a global hospitality and F&B group operating hundreds of hotels and thousands of restaurants worldwide.
-----
1. A six-year due diligence?

Most M&A buyers learn about a target through an IM, data room and several weeks of due diligence.

Minor had something different: years of operating experience.

It had invested its own capital, operated stores and experienced the unit economics and consumer demand firsthand.

In that sense, Minor’s real due diligence may not have started in 2026.
-----
2. It may have started in 2019.

Why a strategic buyer rather than PE?

PE investors reportedly showed interest in Bonchon as well. We don’t know the competing bids, but their valuation logic would have been different.

A financial buyer asks:

“How much can this business generate, and what return can we achieve at exit?”

Minor could ask:

“How much can this business generate under our ownership?”

Its restaurant network, infrastructure and international footprint could create value unavailable to a financial buyer.
-----
3. Did VIG build the business — or the buyer?

This may be the most interesting lesson from the deal.

When VIG invested in Bonchon in 2018, Minor wasn’t the buyer. But as Bonchon expanded internationally, Minor evolved from operator to master franchisee and ultimately to strategic acquirer.

We often think of PE value creation as revenue growth, EBITDA improvement and geographic expansion.

Perhaps there is another dimension:

Building not only the value of the company, but also the buyer who can value it most.

Distributors, JV partners, licensees and master franchisees can become powerful strategic buyers because they understand the business and possess unique synergies.

Bonchon’s buyer may not have appeared when the sale process began.

It may have been developing for the previous six years.
