🚨 Major shift happening in Korea's SME landscape - and it's creating unprecedented opportunities for international investors

Korean small and medium-sized business owners are increasingly putting their companies up for sale, driven by crushing inheritance tax rates up to 50% making family succession nearly impossible.

The numbers tell the story: According to BridgeCode, an M&A advisory firm specializing in SMEs, M&A inquiries have surged 50% in just one year - from 1,600 to an expected 2,400 cases in 2025. Most telling? 60% of these inquiries come from manufacturers holding critical technologies.

📊 Scale of opportunity
Korea has 8.04 million SMEs employing nearly 19 million workers. Many of these companies possess specialized "root technologies" in casting, mold-making, plastic processing, welding, surface treatment, and heat treatment.

💡 What this means for international players:
This isn't just a crisis - it's a strategic window. Korean SMEs with proven technologies and established operations are becoming available at potentially attractive valuations. For overseas companies looking to:
• Gain access to advanced manufacturing capabilities
• Enter the Korean market with established operations 
• Acquire specialized technologies and skilled workforces

The timing couldn't be more compelling. While Korean business owners face succession challenges, international investors have an opportunity to acquire quality assets in one of Asia's most developed industrial ecosystems.

The question isn't whether this trend will continue - it's whether international companies will recognize and act on this historic opportunity.
