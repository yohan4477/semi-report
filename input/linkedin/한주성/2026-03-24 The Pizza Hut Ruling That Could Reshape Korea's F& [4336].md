<The Pizza Hut Ruling That Could Reshape Korea's F&B Franchise Industry>

In January 2025, South Korea's Supreme Court ordered Pizza Hut Korea to refund ₩21.5 billion in supply chain markups collected from franchisees for years.

The industry hasn't been the same since. Lawsuits are filed or pending against 14+ brands — BHC, BBQ, Burger King, Baskin-Robbins, A Twosome Place, Kyochon, Gupne Chicken. Baskin-Robbins alone has 417 plaintiffs. Mega Coffee and The Venti are expected to add 1,000+ more by late March. That franchisors are retaining Kim & Chang, Bae Kim & Lee, and Lee & Ko tells you this goes beyond a paperwork fix.

1️⃣ This Isn't a Disclosure Problem. It's a Business Model Problem

The easy read: franchisors forgot to put the right language in their contracts. The Supreme Court raised a harder question. Its "specific mutual consent" standard runs four tests: Was the markup disclosed? Was the method defined? Did franchisees genuinely consent? And — was the margin itself reasonable?

That last point is explosive. Even a disclosed markup can be voided under Korea's Act on Regulation of Terms and Conditions if deemed excessive. Disclosure is not a safe harbor.
What's under scrutiny is the industry's de facto hidden royalty — minimal headline royalties to recruit franchisees, real margin extracted through mandatory supply markups. Courts are now asking whether that model holds up.

2️⃣ The M&A Implications

EBITDA may be overstated. If markups are clawed back, the earnings you underwrote don't fully exist.

Contingent liabilities are hard to size. Years of recoverable past markups hinge on statute of limitations interpretation — still open at close.
PE exits just got harder. Exposed sponsors face tougher indemnity talks and valuation haircuts.

3️⃣ DD Imperatives Going Forward

Disaggregate supply markup from revenue. If you can't isolate this line in the EBITDA build, your valuation is built on sand.

Expand legal review. A reasonableness assessment of the margin methodology under Korea's terms and conditions regulation is now table stakes.

Screen for latent litigation. The 14 current defendants won't be the last. Similar supply structures warrant a class action stress test.
Re-underwrite without the markup. Model the scenario where it disappears entirely. That downside is the real investment thesis test.

4️⃣ The Bottom Line

Strip the supply markup from EBITDA. What remains is your actual starting point — and how courts rule on these cases may force a structural rethink of how Korean franchisors make money for decades to come.
