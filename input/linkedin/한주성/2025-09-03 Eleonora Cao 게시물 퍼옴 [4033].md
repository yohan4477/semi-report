The Death of the PMO?

For two decades, the Project Management Office was sold as the guardian of delivery.
Dashboards. Templates. Stage gates. Reports.

But if PMOs really guaranteed success, we wouldn’t keep watching billion-dollar projects unravel in slow motion.

Here is my view ….
Most PMOs became reporting factories, not governance engines.
They tracked tasks instead of interrogating assumptions.
They escalated risks with no teeth to act.
They produced “traffic lights” that reassured executives instead of challenging them.

In practice, the PMO became a bureaucratic machine:
 • Endless templates and checklists.
 • Reports created to feed the process, not to change decisions.
 • Stage gates ticked off like box-ticking rituals.
 • Metrics chosen for what’s easy to report, not what reveals danger.
 • Hours of experienced people trapped feeding the system rather than fixing the work.

This machine gives leaders a false sense of control. Green dashboards glow, binders pile up, and yet beneath the surface the project drifts, bleeding money and time.

Governance failed because it became retrospective.
It explained failure after the fact instead of preventing it.

And finance is no different.
Quarterly closes and variance analyses don’t save projects…they just write the post-mortem. By the time the numbers confirm reality, it’s already too late.

So is the PMO dead?
Not yet. But the old model is obsolete.

What we need isn’t “more dashboards” or “better alignment.”
We need governance that:
 • Asks the awkward question before the crisis.
 • Catches the smoke before the fire.
 • Challenges the plan before the cement sets.

Dashboards don’t save projects.
Conviction, foresight, and the courage to confront reality by calling BS….BS, do.
