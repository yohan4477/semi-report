[Investment in Korea 101 – Ep. 1: The Market That Was Never for Sale]

At PwC Korea, I advise cross-border M&A transactions with a focus on financial due diligence, valuation, carve-outs, and post-merger integration.
One question comes up more than any other.

"Where are the good mid-sized Korean companies to buy?"

The answer surprises many investors. They're everywhere. Many are highly profitable, well managed and globally competitive. The problem is that, historically, they haven't been for sale. This was never about foreign ownership restrictions. Foreign investors have acquired controlling stakes in Korean businesses for decades.

The real issue was supply. Many of Korea's best founder-led companies were never built with an exit in mind. Their founders didn't see them as assets to monetize. They saw them as life's work - businesses to pass on to the next generation.

As a result, the opportunities foreign buyers wanted most rarely reached the market. After all, you can't buy what nobody wants to sell.

But something important is changing and it's happening faster than most people outside Korea realize. The generation that built much of Korea's industrial economy is reaching retirement at roughly the same time.
Many founders don't have a successor willing - or capable - of taking over.

At the same time, Korea's inheritance tax regime has made succession increasingly difficult for business-owning families. For a growing number of them, selling is no longer viewed as failure. It's becoming the strategy.

The result is something the Korean M&A market hasn't seen in decades:
A growing supply of quality mid-market businesses becoming available for acquisition. The very assets many foreign investors have been looking for all along.

None of this makes Korea easy. And it certainly doesn't make it cheap.
What it does make Korea is investable in a way it hasn't been for a long time. For all the attention Korea receives today - Semiconductor, K-beauty, shipbuilding, defense, advanced manufacturing etc. - surprisingly few foreign investors truly understand how this market works.

That's the gap this series aims to address. The world has noticed Korea.
My goal is to help investors understand what they're actually looking at over a series of episodes. 

If you're evaluating Korea as an investment destination, this series is for you. Follow along.
