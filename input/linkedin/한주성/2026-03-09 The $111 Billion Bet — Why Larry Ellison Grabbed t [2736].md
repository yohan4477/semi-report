<The $111 Billion Bet — Why Larry Ellison Grabbed the Deal Netflix Just Walked Away From>

🔔 Netflix walked away from acquiring Warner Bros. Discovery. Its stock jumped 13%.

That's not a typo. Investors cheered the exit — because a $111 billion deal means massive debt, integration risk, and years of uncertainty. Netflix is growing fine without it.

But the moment Netflix put it down, Paramount Skydance picked it right back up.

Paramount's market cap? Around $8 billion. So how does an $8 billion company pull off a $111 billion acquisition?

🏗️ Simple. It's not Paramount's money.

The capital comes from $33B in assumed WBD debt, $57.5B in financing from BofA, Citi, and Apollo, sovereign wealth funds from Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE — and the Ellison family.

Which brings us to the real question: whose deal is this?

👨‍👦 This is Larry Ellison's deal.

David Ellison (son) owns Skydance → acquired Paramount → now acquiring WBD. The financial backbone traces directly to his father, Larry Ellison, Oracle co-founder with a net worth north of $150 billion.

A father-and-son project. With a media empire as the endgame.

✅ The real synergies:

• Warner brings HBO, DC, Harry Potter. Paramount brings CBS, Nickelodeon, SpongeBob. Combined: every age group, every genre.
• 210M combined subscribers vs. Netflix's 325M — still behind, but now at fighting scale.
• Cross-demographic ad packages command serious pricing premiums.
• Warner + Paramount on Oracle Cloud = billions in AWS/Azure fees staying inside the group.

⚠️ The risks are real — and history is not encouraging:

• Warner Bros. has already survived two painful mergers. AT&T's 2018 acquisition was widely called one of the worst media deals in history. The Discovery merger brought its own chaos, talent exodus, and writedowns. The scars are still fresh.
• Platform consolidation always bleeds subscribers. Merging HBO Max and Paramount+ will push out users who signed up for one, not the other. Brand loyalty doesn't transfer automatically.
• The streaming market is maturing fast. Netflix had to introduce ads and crack down on password sharing just to sustain growth. For a late challenger, the runway is shorter and the climb is steeper.

💭 So what's Larry really after?

Oracle sits at #4 in cloud, behind AWS, Azure, and Google. Locking in Warner and Paramount as anchor customers structurally strengthens Oracle's position as AI drives cloud demand through the roof.

But at 80+ years old with $150B in the bank? This probably isn't just about cloud market share.

Gates defined the PC era. Jobs opened the smartphone age. Musk is reaching for Mars.

Larry Ellison built a database empire. Now he seems to be asking: what does he want to be remembered for?

$111 billion might just be his answer.
