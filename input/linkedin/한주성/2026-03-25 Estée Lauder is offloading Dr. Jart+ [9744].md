<Estée Lauder is offloading Dr. Jart+>

Acquisition valuation: roughly $1.5 billion. Current asking price: under $200 million.

Ninety percent of enterprise value — gone in seven years.

🌊 Same Name, Different Game

The brands winning right now are Anua, Beauty of Joseon, COSRX, Medicube. Indie. Scrappy. Algorithmically native. Built for TikTok, not department stores.

Dr. Jart+ is a different generation. It rose in the early 2010s as a premium derma-cosmetic brand, powered by Chinese tourists and duty-free retail.

When Estée Lauder acquired it in 2019, the ground was already shifting. Chinese consumers pivoted to C-beauty. Duty-free collapsed under COVID. The new K-beauty boom is driven by the U.S. and Europe. Dr. Jart+ missed that wave entirely.

⛓️ Why It Couldn't Pivot

Channel lock-in was the first trap. A brand built on department stores can't migrate to Amazon and TikTok Shop without triggering retailer backlash. Cut prices online, and your department store buyer quietly demotes your shelf.

Speed was the second. Indie brands go from trend to launch in three to six months. Conglomerates run on a lengthier cycle. By the time approvals clear, the moment has passed.

Then the founder left — and with him, the authenticity that made the brand viral in the first place.

💡 So Where Does Estée Lauder Go?

Two paths forward, and they're not mutually exclusive.

Double down on luxury. Estée Lauder owns La Mer, Jo Malone, Le Labo, Tom Ford Beauty, Kilian Paris. No TikTok brand can manufacture sixty years of heritage. That territory is structurally protected.

Become infrastructure. Its 140+ country distribution, FDA and EC expertise, and Sephora relationships are exactly what indie brands lack. Licensing that — rather than competing — is a real business model. L'Oréal is already moving this way.

The real mistake wasn't buying Dr. Jart+.
It was spending seven years fighting a battle it was never built to win.

Brands chase trends. Platforms own them.
